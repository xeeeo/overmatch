# Overmatch – Design Pillars

Overmatch is a spiritual successor to Command & Conquer: Generals – Zero Hour. These pillars are the test for every feature: if it doesn't serve one of them, it waits.

## 1. Build anywhere
Builder units (Dozer, Engineering Vehicle, Worker) construct buildings anywhere they can reach. No build radius, no sidebar. Bases sprawl, forward bases are a strategy, and a lone builder behind enemy lines is a threat.

## 2. The supply-pile economy
Finite supply piles near each start and in contested spots. Harvesters shuttle between piles and a Supply Center. Each faction has a sustainable trickle building and can capture Oil Derricks. Power gates production for two of the three factions. Running out of piles forces expansion or a decisive push.

## 3. Hard asymmetry
The three factions do not mirror. Coalition is expensive and precise. Directorate is mass and armour. Network is cheap, sneaky and improvised. Each has its own economy quirk, tech path, powers and superweapon. Counters live in a damage-type × armour-type table, and every unit has a job.

## 4. Promotion powers and superweapons
Kills earn promotion points; spend them on general's powers with cooldowns. Superweapons are late-game buildings on a long visible countdown that everyone is warned about. Both exist to force action.

## 5. Tempo
Games are fast. Units move quickly, die quickly, and explode spectacularly. Rushes are viable and defensible. A skirmish is 15–30 minutes.

## 6. Tone
Deadly serious with a straight face that occasionally cracks. Units talk, a lot. News-ticker briefings, dry commander voice, dark humour in the voice lines. It is a game about the world's ugliest tools of war; it does not pretend otherwise, and it does not glorify any real nation or group.

## 7. The 2030s battlefield
This is the sequel's hook: the modern reality that Generals guessed at in 2003, as it actually turned out.
- Cheap drones everywhere: FPV strikes, loitering munitions, drone carriers, drone defence.
- Electronic warfare: jamming that blinds radar, downs drones, and breaks guidance.
- Cyber: disabling buildings, stealing funds, spoofing.
- Long-range precision: HIMARS-style rocket artillery, cruise and ballistic missiles, hypersonics.
- Hybrid war: PMCs, insurgent networks, civilian infrastructure as a weapon.
- Cheap mass versus exquisite tech: the central strategic tension, mirrored in the faction triangle.

## 8. Data-driven and moddable
Every unit, building, weapon, upgrade, power and AI behaviour is a text file. The sim is separate from the engine. Modding is editing a folder.

## 9. Open
GPLv3 code, CC BY 4.0 assets, every model regenerated from a script, public repo, CI builds for both platforms.

## Non-goals for v1
- Campaign or story missions.
- Multiplayer (the fixed-tick sim keeps it possible later).
- Sub-generals and Generals' Challenge (planned extension).
- Realistic graphics. Low-poly stylised is the look.
